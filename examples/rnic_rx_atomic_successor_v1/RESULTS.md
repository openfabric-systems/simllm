## Boundary and independent arithmetic

The public clock has period 5000 ps and phase 2500 ps. At 96.6 Gb/s, each
clock earns exactly60.375 bytes, represented with an8e12-unit denominator per
byte. A21-byte head arriving at2500 ps drains at7500 ps, leaving39.375 earned
bytes. A64-byte successor admitted at7500 ps waits for12500 ps, when99.75
bytes afford its full debit. Both widths64 and128 give this result.

Service precedes admission, and no packet is served twice at one timestamp.
The intermediate empty state has zero duration. A private candidate keeps the
earned remainder without becoming live credit, occupancy, physical work or a
scheduled event. Only an actual successful same-time admission moves it to
live credit. Invalid envelopes and capacity/rate discards do not activate it.
Existing admitted sequence and duplicate rejections retain their ingress charge.

An arrival at7501 ps sees a positive empty interval and finishes at17500 ps.
A true idle successor at12500 ps finishes at22500 ps. Already queued and
7499-ps successors finish at12500 ps. Repeated queries and progress calls
cannot accrue or debit twice. The original arbitrary monotone picosecond input
domain remains intact.

The metric is the successor's final receive-service debit timestamp, with no

The independent clock-index bound is
`max(ceil(106/refill_bytes), 1+ceil(85/width_bytes))`; the timestamp is

Increasing rate or width cannot worsen these measured cells; geometry
saturation explains the equal upper-rate results. No universal inverse-rate
scaling is claimed for this short workload.
